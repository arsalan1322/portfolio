Arsalan Khan

I'm a master's student at the Institute for Quantum Computing (IQC), University of Waterloo, supervised by Professors Thomas Jennewein and Michal Bajcsy. I study quantum communication and photonics, with a particular interest in generating, controlling, and distributing entanglement.

My work spans entangled-photon architectures, automated experiments, and photon-source characterization. I'm also a Quantum Optics Student at the Canadian Space Agency. Previously, I worked on nonlinear quantum photonics at the University of Toronto and spent a year in optical verification engineering at Lumentum.

I want to help build the quantum internet and take a leading role in deciding how it develops. My work in satellite quantum communication and entanglement generation has given me a starting point. Next, during my PhD, I want to explore how quantum memories, on-chip photon sources, and new network architectures can bring us closer to networks people can actually use. In the future, I see myself leading a research group or technical team working toward that goal, while helping shape the decisions that bring these networks into practice.

Beyond research, I'm co-chair of the Canadian Graduate Quantum Conference 2027, helping lead a national gathering planned for about 100 graduate researchers at IQC and Perimeter Institute. I also taught in IQC's undergraduate quantum summer school and share quantum science with younger students through hands-on outreach.

In my free time I enjoy exploring my creativity in other avenues such as drawing and have recently taken up sewing and designing my own clothes. Music is another big part of my life: I love exploring experimental genres, and I'm starting to learn how to produce my own.
